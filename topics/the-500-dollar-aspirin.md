## 1. Core Thesis & Nuance

### The Core Premise
Organizations frequently hire expensive external consulting firms to solve problems their internal engineering team diagnosed months or years prior. Leadership often pays 10x–50x more for the "external stamp of approval," demoralizing in-house talent and creating a culture where internal credibility is diminished until someone from the outside says it.

### The Counter-Perspective (Steel-Manning Leadership's Side)
To write a fair and impactful piece, we must address why leadership actually does this:
1. **Liability & Political Cover**: External consultants provide a "CYA" safety net for leadership. If the project fails, they can blame McKinsey/Accenture; if an internal initiative fails, the VP's job is on the line.
2. **Executive Synthesis Gap**: In-house engineers often present problems in terms of technical debt, code hygiene, or architectural purity, whereas consultants package the exact same solution in terms of risk mitigation, ROI, and EBITDA impact.
3. **Internal Deadlocks**: Multiple internal engineering teams often disagree on the solution due to siloed interests. An external party breaks the political stalemate.

### Blind Spots & Nuances to Explore
- Where do in-house teams contribute to this problem? (e.g., complaining without presenting actionable, business-aligned roadmaps).
- How can senior/lead engineers communicate so they *become* the trusted advisors executives listen to?

## 2. Evidence & Story Bank

### Personal Anecdotes
- Experiences where leadership paid exorbitant vendor contracts for recommendations that matched internal engineering post-mortems or proposals word-for-word.
- The morale drop when the engineering team is asked to execute the consultant's slide deck.

### External References & Concepts
- The *Prophet in Their Own Land* syndrome (familiarity breeds devaluation).
- Conway's Law and corporate signaling theory.
- The concept of "Expensive Advice" bias: When organizations pay a high price for an opinion, they are psychologically primed to value and act upon it.

### Visual Analogies & Metaphors
- **The $500 Aspirin**: Going to the emergency room for a simple headache when the medicine cabinet at home had the exact same pill for 10 cents—because you only trusted the doctor in the white coat to diagnose it.
